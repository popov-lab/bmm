## Decision

### D0 Capacity and calendar

The maintainer has 4–6 hours a week for reading reviews and merging, 6 or more for writing and 3 or more
for the companions, until 2027-03-31; nothing is merged between 20 December 2026 and 6 January 2027; the
teaching block in February and March reduces capacity. The TeaP 2027 workshop is the Sunday before the
conference, **21 March 2027**, 10:00–17:00 (TeaP runs 21–24 March in Mannheim; the organisers have not
yet confirmed the slot). The release it teaches from must be installed from CRAN by **21 February 2027**.
The review budget to the freeze is about 37 hours; the fifteen reviewed pull requests cost about 12–15 of
them.

### D1 What 1.5.0 is for: the contract release

1.5.0 ships the model contract and the work that is reviewed: `model_spec()` (#633), `simulate_data()`
(#634), `link_transform()` (#635), the circular mixture rebuild (#434, #583–#590), `sdm()` activation,
scaling and option sets (#558, #637, #641), `psychometric_function()` (#507), trial-level data for the
count models (ADR 0006 T1–T4 and T2b: #648 merged, #661, #665, #667, the probit-GLMM article), population
summaries (#535), and the small items (#444, #525, #556, #564, #664). TCC (#566) and ezcdm (#524) are in
only if their pilot reviews are posted and merged by 18 December; change detection (#136) moves to 1.6.0.
Both companions hang on the contract, and the workshop needs a stable CRAN release more than one more
model.

### D2 Dates for 1.5.0, and what yields first

Feature freeze Friday 15 January 2027, review complete and release branch Friday 29 January, CRAN
submission Monday 1 February, bounce buffer to Monday 15 February. Milestone 1.5.0 moves from 15 December
to 15 January. If review time runs short, items drop in this order: change detection, TCC, #535, T2b
(#667), `sdm()` option sets (#641). The contract, the circular mixture stack, #507, #661 and #665 never
yield.

### D3 Validation as a release gate, phased

1.5.0 is tagged only with a green bmmtools validation card (recovery and SBC) for every new or rebuilt
model: mixture2p, mixture3p, imm, sdm, psychometric_function, and the trial-level layouts of the SDT and
MPT models (TCC and ezcdm if they are in). From 1.6.0 every model needs a card, produced by a scheduled
`cards` workflow in GidonFrischkorn/bmmtools that publishes a card site; the maintainer reads its failures
and files them as bmmtools issues that link the bmm model. The label `validation-gate` marks the bmm pull
requests the gate holds.

### D4 Default priors

From 1.5.0 every change to a default prior is listed under `### Changes to default priors` in its release
section and labelled `prior-change`; defaults never change inside a minor line (1.5.x), only in a minor
release. 1.6.0 adds a prior-set version to every fit and `bmm_options(prior_version = )` to reproduce an
older default set. #458 closes with #590, which carries #630; fits labelled 0.4.5.9000–0.4.7.9000 stay
unsupported (`?restructure` says so) and are filed as Future.

### D5 Race models as planned; across-trial variability later

1.6.0 ships LBA (#500), LNR (#501), RDM (#502), the LBA drift kernels (#565) and cswald `sndt` (#406).
Each needs a base merge of `dev-1.6.0`, a `simulate_data()` method, the #554 vocabulary,
`.check_constructor_dots()`, a fresh review (the October reviews are stale) and a card. #435 (across-trial
variability for `ddm()`) stays Future; a feasibility spike in December 2026 measures the cost per gradient
of brms's `wiener` with `sv`, `sz` and `st0` and the parity with rtdists, and the 1.6.0 scope check on
15 February decides.

### D6 Organisation

bmmtools and bmm-explorer stay under GidonFrischkorn; a move is reconsidered when bmmtools nears CRAN. The
other maintainer is asked for the one-line consent that ADR 0005 requires, and the two jobs in

changed, what was decided. This applies to the fifteen reviewed pull requests too.

## Amendment 2026-11-11 (#699): code health after 1.5.0

**Context.** A read-only review of `develop` in November 2026 found the package's code cleaner than brms on
long functions, function length and duplication, but over-decomposed: 411 internal helpers with one caller, 121
of them called from another one-caller helper. It also found roxygen blocks on internal functions, 279 banner
comment lines, and a test suite of 44,049 lines whose style costs CI time and review time: one scenario per
block, mock fits that assert only silence, 1,151 expectations on message wording, tests pinned to internal
function names. It found three latent risks: an internal `identical()` generic whose formula methods call a
Suggests package without a guard, six exported distribution functions documented as working that only stop,
and two rules for a user data column that collides with a reserved name (the SDT models overwrite it with a
warning, m3 and mpt stop).

**Decision.**

- *Decide before 1.5.0, build after.* All eight pull requests open into `develop` on 7 November 2026 changed
  files the cleanup would edit, and each carried a review of its head; a cleanup before the freeze would turn
  those reviews stale and spend the review budget of D0 twice. 1.5.0 therefore carries only the development
  rules in `AGENTS.md` (documentation, conflicting with nothing) and two contract-surface fixes: the
  `identical()` override is removed and the `fs` import is replaced by base R. The six stubbed distribution
  functions are fixed under #556, already in the 1.5.0 scope (D1).
- *After 1.5.0 is on CRAN and `dev-1.6.0` has been merged into `develop`* (0002), the comment and test hygiene
  passes land first, before the 1.6.0 stacks are refreshed onto `develop`, so that each stack absorbs them in
  the one merge it needs anyway. Each pass is produced by a script that any branch can rerun on itself. The
  structural refactors follow, family by family, each after the 1.6.0 work that edits the same files has
  landed.
- *Behaviour.* The cleanup changes nothing a user sees, with three exceptions decided here: the reserved-column
  rule becomes one rule (stop, as m3 and mpt do) in 1.6.0; `rtdists` moves to Suggests in 1.7.0; and bmm's
  errors and warnings gain condition classes in 1.6.0, for packages that catch them. Every pull request shows,
  on its base and its head, that emitted Stan code, Stan data, default priors, post-processing on the test
  fixtures and the exported surface are unchanged; its review reads the diff for changed messages and
  warnings.
- *Dates.*

  | Date | Gate |
  |---|---|
  | Fri 18 Dec 2026 | the two contract-surface fixes merged into `develop`, else they move to 1.6.0 |
  | Fri 26 Feb 2027 | the comment and test hygiene passes merged into `develop`, else they wait until after the 1.6.0 submission |
  | Fri 16 Apr 2027 | no structural refactor merges from this date until the 1.6.0 submission (Tue 1 Jun 2027) |

- *Yield rule for 1.6.0* (extends D2): if review time runs short, the structural refactors yield before any
  1.6.0 feature; the comment and test hygiene passes do not yield, because they lower the CI and review cost
  of everything else. Refactors not merged by the 1.6.0 submission continue after it without a release
  attached.
- *Enforcement.* `test-conventions.R` pins banner comment lines and doubly tagged internal roxygen at zero once
  the comment pass has removed them, and ratchets the number of test titles that name an internal function
  at its count after the test pass. An advisory script, `.github/scripts/helper-callers.R`, lists internal
  helpers with fewer callers than the `AGENTS.md` rule asks; it reports and does not fail CI.

**Consequences.** About 4,500 fewer test lines, about 2,100 fewer lines in `R/` and fewer mock fits per CI
run are expected by mid-2027; open branches pay one scripted rerun at their next merge of `develop`. The
workstream is tracked in one issue, #699 (label `code-health`); each pull request lists what it changes.
